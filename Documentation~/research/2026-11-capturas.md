## 2026-11-02 · Ultra Water: an eikonal shore-wave field (`shoreMap.js`)
- **Type and origin:** link, GitHub repo https://github.com/Humpalumps/Ultra-Water (Humpalumps, created 2026-10-28, MIT, three.js / WebGL2). Read from the README and file tree; `shoreMap.js` itself not yet read line by line.
- **What Claudio said:** «me interesa lo de shoreMap, para que lo anotes en las ideas pendientes de Islands. No sé si también servirá para el flujo de las nubes? De acuerdo con el shader, probablemente hay una mejor forma en Unity.»
- **What it is:** a real-time FFT ocean renderer (Tessendorf, 4 cascades), shore breakers, foam, caustics, underwater pass and buoyancy. Performance figures are the author's own (200+ fps on an RTX 3060, 60 fps on a mid-range phone), not measured here. The README says the design was argued out with a second agent and that earlier figures were 2–4× off: young code, treat as unproven.
- **The part that matters for Islands:** `src/water/shoreMap.js` computes a **travel-time field** by fast sweeping from the coast with speed c = √(g·depth), then derives wave direction, front curvature and a ray-tube shoaling factor. Fronts slow down, bunch up and refract around the bathymetry; coasts facing the swell get bigger sets; where fronts from two coast stretches meet (shocks) the waves fade out. It is a grid algorithm, deterministic and GPU-free, the same family as the distance-map validators (backlog n.º 1, `BfsDistanceField`), but with a continuous metric (Eikonal) instead of BFS steps.
- 💡 **Use here:** a **wave-exposure field** after `Stage_Shore2D`: per coast cell, how much swell arrives and from where. It would drive beaches on exposed coasts, sheltered coves and harbours, cliffs and sea stacks where energy is high, and where the sea people would beach canoes. Needs a bathymetry (sea-depth) field; the terrain height below the water level may already serve. Rendering stays out: Unity has its own water (HDRP Water System, flow maps), so the shader is not worth porting.
- 💡 **Clouds / moisture (Claudio's question):** NoiseLab v3's wind (`WindField` in `Samples/0.1.0-preview/NoiseLab/`) is already a sweep: one direction for the whole map, air carried line by line upwind. Fast sweeping is the generalisation when the wind **changes direction per cell** (deflected by ridges, funnelled through passes): solve the arrival time of sea air and carry its humidity along the characteristics, as `shoreMap.js` carries direction and shoaling. Caveats: the eikonal picks the *fastest* path, so air goes around peaks instead of over them (plausible for low air, wrong for orographic rain, which needs the climb); and wind needs an **anisotropic** speed (faster downwind), which plain fast sweeping does not handle without changes (ordered upwind methods, Sethian & Vladimirsky 2003). Moving clouds on screen are rendering, not generation: Unity side.
- **Sources:** Tessendorf, *Simulating Ocean Water* (SIGGRAPH 2001); Zhao, «A fast sweeping method for Eikonal equations», *Math. Comp.* 74 (2005); Evan Wallace, *WebGL Water* (caustics).

- **Read in full (transcripts):** 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6; 7 was already carded; 8 has no speech (drone footage only).
- **Card, video 3 (cold-air pools):** at night the ground radiates heat, the air above it gets denser and drains downhill "like water" (1:45–2:05). In closed depressions it cannot leave: limestone sinkholes stay holes because water seeps into the rock and no stream cuts an outlet (2:38–3:30). Result: the Grünloch (Austria) recorded −52.6 °C, up to 30 °C colder than its rim, with a **reversed tree line** (trees shrink downslope until only alpine plants remain at the bottom, 0:42–1:02); Peter Sinks (Utah) −56.3 °C. Semi-closed valleys do it milder (California's Central Valley: orange groves on the upper slopes, valley floor one or two hardiness zones colder, 5:00–6:20). Endorheic basins and lava-dammed plains do the same (7:00–8:35).
- **Card, video 2 (tropical freezes):** cold-air damming: a shallow, dense cold air mass pools against the east side of a north–south range and is pushed toward the equator (5:50–6:45). Works best with a steep range plus a flat plain against it (the Andes and the Chaco: the *friaje*, 11:00–14:20; 2003, ~13,000 cases of severe hypothermia in Peru per the video). After a freeze, epiphytes die; mountain microclimates act as **refugia** for recolonisation (9:12–9:50). Single canopies shield seedlings from radiative frost (**nurse trees**, 18:30–19:30).
- **Card, video 5 (chinampas):** artificial raised beds in a shallow lake of an endorheic basin: willow-stake perimeter, woven reeds, plant matter and lake mud in layers; the willows (*ahuejotes*) root and fix the edge (4:44–5:40). Max ~12 m wide so every side touches water; three canal sizes (barge, canoe, irrigation-only) (7:17–8:20). Today ~2,200 ha left of "more than a hundred thousand" (a claim by the Arca Tierra founder, unverified; 10:07–10:17).
- **Card, video 1 (biggest canyon):** "biggest" depends on the metric: deepest Yarlung Tsangpo (~6,000 m, 500+ km); Colca and Cotahuasi (Peru) over 3,000 m from minor rivers, because they sit inside the Andes (1:55–2:18); longest is under the Greenland ice (750 km); submarine canyons are carved by dense, sediment-laden flows down the shelf (Congo, Hudson 640 km); largest area Bering Canyon (~30,000 km²), largest volume Zhemchug (~5,800 km³; the video says "3,000 kilometers" deep, a slip for ~2.6 km). Lesson for a generator: canyon depth comes from relief available (mountains), not river size.
- **Card, video 6 (Malé):** ~210,000 people on a 1.9 × 1.2 km island, over 40 % of the Maldives (5,000 in the 1920s); density from historical inertia (sultanate seat, first mosques) plus centralised services (hospitals, airport, port). ~1,000+ islands, ~190 inhabited, highest point 5.1 m, 80 % of islands under 1 m; aquifers salted, water from rain, desalination or imports; land reclamation (Hulhumalé) as the plan against the sea (10:40–13:50).
- **Card, video 4 (10,000 years ago, world tour):** geography-relevant bits: post-glacial sea-level rise (~7.5 m in 160 years per the video, 2:43) drowning Doggerland, Sunda and Sahul; a ~1 km stone wall in the Baltic to drive reindeer (4:32); the Green Sahara as a recurring cycle (19:52–20:24). Human-history content goes to cuaderno-civilizaciones.
- 💡 **Possible use here:**

  - **Sea level as a time parameter** (videos 4 and 6): one map at several sea levels (glacial, today, +1 m) is cheap with the percentage water threshold already in NoiseLab; exposes drowned land bridges and low atolls that vanish.
  - **Cold-air pooling term in `Stage_Biome2D`.** Today temperature only falls with height (`biomeLapseRate`, `PCGMapTilemapVisualization.cs:218`). Once `FillDepth` from Priority-Flood exists ([`playlist-pcg/backlog.md`](playlist-pcg/backlog.md) l. 54), subtract `k · FillDepth` (depth of the closed basin under its spill point) from temperature on dry depressions.
